USE CASE · MYANMAR NATIONAL QR (MMQR)

Paying by QR — AYA Pay on the MyanmarPay rail

A customer at a shop counter opens AYA Pay and scans the MyanmarPay standee beside the till. Behind that one tap sit a central-bank scheme, a national switch, two institutions and a real-time credit. This page maps exactly that path — from camera to settlement — using only what the public record supports; anything it does not state is drawn dashed.

SECTION 01 · THE ARCHITECTURE

Five zones, two paths, one settlement layer

Left to right: the customer, AYA Pay as the Originating Institute (OI), the national layer — scheme above, switch below — the merchant’s Receiving Institute (RI), and the merchant at the counter. Path ② — off-us / interop — is the story of this page, drawn with its nine steps numbered; path ① is the quieter on-us loop that never leaves AYA. Solid means the public record states it; dashed means it does not.

path ② · off-us / interop (numbered 1–9) path ① · on-us, inside AYA confirmations governance & settlement verified / public not public — inferred boundary
1Scan — the camera reads the printed standee; no network involved yet
2Parse — tag 26: GUID MM.COM.MMQR + the 15-digit Merchant ID
3Confirm — amount typed (static QR carries none), PIN entered
4Debit — AYA Pay, as OI, debits the customer’s wallet ledger
5Instruct — payment instruction to the Digital Payment Switch (message semantics & format — not public)
6Route — RTRP resolves the Merchant ID to the merchant’s RI (routing map — not public)
7Credit — the RI credits the merchant’s account in real time
8Confirm, both sides — receipt in the payer’s app; alert in the AYA Pay Merchant app (SMS — dashed side-channel)
9Settle — between the institutions across CBM settlement accounts (net vs gross, windows — not public)
Reading the diagram: the transaction itself is the navy path — nine numbered steps from the camera to the central bank. Everything the customer feels is over by step 8; step 9 is between institutions and invisible at the counter. The scheme (amber, top) writes the rules the boxes play by; the settlement layer (amber, bottom) is where the two institutions square up. The dashed boxes inside the switch are deliberate: the public record names the switch, its operator and its real-time subsystem, but not its message formats or its routing table.
Confidence convention, used across the whole page: solid = a named public source states it (spec, Code of Conduct, operator or bank site, government press). Dashed = not public — the boundary is real but its mechanics are undocumented; every dashed item is listed verbatim in the Notes.

SECTION 02 · INSIDE THE QR

What the standee actually says

An MMQR standee is a few hundred characters of EMVCo-structured text. Tag 26 is the payload that matters — the scheme identifier and the 15-digit Merchant ID that step 6 routes on — and tag 64 is the MMQR signature move: a local-language merchant name is mandatory, not optional. All fields below are from the official Myanmar QR Code Specification (May 2023).

Illustrative standee — the modules are decorative, not a scannable code. A real plate carries the merchant’s name in English (tag 59) and in Myanmar Unicode (tag 64).

Why two of these fields carry the story: tag 26 is what makes one printed plate reachable from fifteen different wallets — the switch resolves its Merchant ID to the right institution — and the conditional tag 54 is why the customer types the amount at a static standee but not at a dynamic till code. Note the spec tension the diagram already drew dashed: dynamic codes are fully defined in the standard, but the switch’s published service list names static QR generation only.

SECTION 03 · FUNDING THE WALLET

Cash in, cash out — the agent loop

The wallet the QR path debits is funded mostly in cash, over the counter of an agent network AYA puts at 38,000+ agents. The two six-step routines below are AYA’s own published instructions, verbatim in structure; here the SMS confirmation is AYA’s own copy — it belongs to the agent transaction, not to the QR payment path of Section 01.

Cash-inDeposit at an agent
  1. Go to the nearest AYA Pay agent — shop counters, marked with the agent sign.
  2. State a deposit — tell the agent you want to cash in to your wallet.
  3. Share the registered mobile number — the number your wallet runs on.
  4. Tell the agent the amount to deposit.
  5. Hand the cash to the agent.
  6. The agent completes the transaction — you receive an SMS confirmation and a receipt; the balance is in the wallet.
Cash-outWithdraw at an agent
  1. Go to the nearest AYA Pay agent — the same counters work both ways.
  2. State a withdrawal — tell the agent you want to cash out of your wallet.
  3. Share the registered mobile number — the wallet to draw from.
  4. Tell the agent the amount to withdraw.
  5. The agent completes the transaction — the wallet is debited.
  6. Receive the cash — with an SMS confirmation and a receipt to match.
One SMS is not the other. The SMS above is part of AYA’s own agent cash-in / cash-out flow. AYA Bank separately sells an “SMS Alert” service — a paid, opt-in notification product on AYA bank accounts (monthly fee, thresholds, branch sign-up). That product is not this flow, and neither is evidence of SMS confirmations on the QR payment path — which is why Section 01 draws SMS as a dashed side-channel.

SECTION 04 · ROLES & RULEBOOK

Who is who on the rail

The scheme’s own vocabulary, from the MyanmarPay Code of Conduct — Originating and Receiving Institute, not issuer and acquirer — plus the two-word distinction that decides whether a payment crosses the switch at all.

The scheme
MyanmarPay

Owned and regulated by the Central Bank of Myanmar — the Code of Conduct names the CBM as “system owner”, with oversight, policy, audit and participant-certification authority. The rulebook covers participation, disputes, settlement obligations and the MDR.

The operator
PayPlus Co., Ltd · Digital Payment Switch

Awarded Digital Payment Switch Operator by the CBM (2021); mandated to “manage, operate, and maintain the technical and operational infrastructure”. Technical responsibility attributed to Andaman Capital Partners — per a CBM Governor statement carried by the Ministry of Information.

OI · in this use case: AYA Pay
Originating Institute

“Financial institution that initiates the transaction on behalf of the payer” — the wallet that scans, checks and debits. Direct participants are “licensed commercial banks, payment service providers, and mobile financial service providers authorized by the Central Bank of Myanmar”.

RI · the merchant’s side
Receiving Institute

“The financial institution that receives the funds on behalf of the payee” — the merchant’s bank or PSP, obliged to credit in real time. Non-bank participants settle behind a settlement bank holding their reserve at the CBM.

1ON-US · Code of Conduct, verbatim

“Transactions in which both the issuing and acquiring institutions belong to the same entity, resulting in faster processing times and potentially lower fees for the merchant.”

2OFF-US · Code of Conduct, verbatim

“Transactions in which the issuing and acquiring institutions are different, requiring the involvement of external networks for processing and settlement.”
